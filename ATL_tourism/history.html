A brief summary of Atlanta's history

Long before the city existed, the area was home to the Muscogee (Creek) and Cherokee peoples, who were forcibly removed in the 1820s and 1830s. Atlanta began in 1837 as "Terminus," the southern end point of the Western & Atlantic Railroad. The settlement was renamed Marthasville in 1843 and became Atlanta in 1845, and it was incorporated as a city in 1847. Its location at the junction of several rail lines made it a fast-growing transportation and commercial center.

That strategic importance made Atlanta a major target during the Civil War. Union forces under General William T. Sherman captured the city in September 1864 after a long campaign, and much of it was burned before Sherman began his March to the Sea. Atlanta rebuilt quickly and became Georgia's state capital in 1868. In the late 1800s it promoted itself as the heart of the "New South," a vision of industry and commerce championed by newspaper editor Henry Grady. Coca-Cola was invented there in 1886, and the 1895 Cotton States and International Exposition showcased the city's ambitions. The same era also brought Jim Crow segregation and racial violence, including the 1906 Atlanta race massacre.

In the 20th century, Atlanta became a center of Black education, business, and culture, anchored by the historically Black colleges of the Atlanta University Center and the Sweet Auburn district. It was the birthplace of Martin Luther King Jr. and a key headquarters of the Civil Rights Movement, including the Southern Christian Leadership Conference, founded in 1957. City leaders marketed Atlanta as "the city too busy to hate." In 1973 Maynard Jackson became the city's first Black mayor, and Atlanta became a symbol of Black political and economic achievement.

In recent decades Atlanta has grown into a major metropolitan and business hub. It is home to companies such as Delta Air Lines, Coca-Cola, and The Home Depot, and Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport ranks among the busiest in the world. The city hosted the 1996 Summer Olympics, which spurred major development downtown. Today metro Atlanta is one of the largest and fastest-growing regions in the U.S. It is known for its film and music industries and its diverse population, and it continues to face challenges such as urban sprawl, traffic, and inequality.
